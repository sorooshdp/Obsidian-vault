# Academic Writing & Pre-Submission Checklist

## Front Matter & Abstract
- [ ] **Write a complete, self-contained Abstract** — state the problem, proposed approach, and key experimental achievements/metrics over baselines; avoid unexplained symbols and citations
- [ ] **Add a Table of Contents** — cover at least top-level sections and primary subsections (levels 1 and 2)
- [ ] **Include a List of Figures and List of Tables** — place immediately after the Table of Contents

## Introduction & Chapter Flow
- [ ] **Structure the Introduction into three core parts** — (1) Problem definition, importance, and applications; (2) Overview of the proposed method; (3) Outline/structure of the report (e.g., *"Chapter 2 covers..., Chapter 3 presents..., and Chapter 4 concludes..."*)
- [ ] **Frame every chapter (from Chapter 2 onward) with an intro and summary** — add 2–3 lines at the start previewing what the chapter covers, and 2–3 lines at the end summarizing key takeaways
- [ ] **Include introductory text between section and subsection headings** — never place a subsection heading (e.g., `1.1`) immediately after a section heading (`1.`) without a brief overview paragraph

## Literature Review, Terminology & Argumentation
- [ ] **Cite every paragraph in Related Work** — never leave a paragraph uncited; if consecutive paragraphs rely on the same source, repeat the citation explicitly so the basis of every statement is clear
- [ ] **Define abbreviations on first use** — write out the full term followed by the abbreviation in parentheses, then use the abbreviation consistently throughout
- [ ] **Introduce technical terms before referencing them** — define concepts, variables, and methods prior to using them in analysis or results
- [ ] **Support every claim with evidence or justification** — present the proof, data, or citation *before* or *immediately alongside* any conclusion; reorder paragraphs where claims appear unsupported
- [ ] **Prefer clear, direct sentences over overly complex ones** — split long multi-clause sentences and verify punctuation to prevent ambiguity

## Figures & Tables
- [ ] **Write concise, descriptive, self-contained captions** — summarize the core takeaway and explain what columns or axes represent so the reader understands the visual without reading the body text
- [ ] **Ensure legible font sizes and high resolution in figures** — verify that axis labels, legends, and annotations remain sharp and readable at standard column/page width
- [ ] **Cite all baseline methods listed in comparison tables** — every external method, dataset, or tool named in a table must have a corresponding citation
- [ ] **Reference every figure and table in the body text** — explicitly call out and discuss each visual (e.g., *"As shown in Fig. 2..."*) before or near where it appears

## Equations & Formalisms
- [ ] **Number all display equations** — assign sequential numbers to every equation used to explain the proposed method
- [ ] **Reference and explain every equation in the text** — cite equations by number (e.g., *"defined in Eq. (3)"*) and define all variables immediately after the formula

## Conclusion, Rigor & References
- [ ] **Write a comprehensive Conclusion** — synthesize main findings/results, explicitly discuss limitations and challenges faced, and outline concrete directions for future work
- [ ] **Validate across diverse datasets and models (for journal/conference papers)** — go beyond a single setup; benchmark across multiple datasets and model architectures with deeper analytical evaluation and clear technical novelty
- [ ] **Audit bibliography completeness and consistency** — verify that every in-text citation matches an entry in the `References` section (and vice versa) and follows the target venue's style guide
