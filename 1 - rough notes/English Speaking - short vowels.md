### Sounds & examples
- **A /æ/**: cat, map, bad
- **E /e/**: bed, pen, ten
- **I /ɪ/**: fit, pig, lip
- **O /ɒ/ or /ɑ/**: pop, nod, stop
- **U /ʌ/**: cup, cut, mud

**Tip:** Short vowels do **not** say their letter names. For short **a**, open mouth wide, tongue flat.

### Rules with examples
1. **One vowel + one syllable → usually short**  
   cat, bed, sit, pop, cup

2. **Vowel + double consonant → short**  
   winning, gripped  
   Contrast: **gripped** = short i / **griped** = long i

3. **Vowel + CK → short**  
   duck, licking  
   Contrast: **licking** = short i / **liking** = long i

4. **Vowel before J/DGE sound → short**  
   badge, bridge

5. **Vowel before TCH sound → short**  
   catch, stitch, fetch

### Important takeaway
If a single vowel is surrounded by consonants—especially double consonants—or comes before **ck, dge/j, tch**, it is usually short.

### Practice tip
Repeat aloud, copy mouth shape, and practice contrast pairs:  
**winning/whining, gripped/griped, licking/liking**.
